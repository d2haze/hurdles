Questions

Frequently asked

What data does the app use?

All split timing data comes directly from The Hurdler's Bible 2 by Ross & Hernandez de Ross — computer analysis of elite race film from competitions and timing boards. The interval ratios, critical zone markers, and maximal velocity figures are all sourced from that work.

For events and times not directly in the source tables (e.g. a custom 14.2s target), the app linearly interpolates between the nearest presets.

Which events are supported?

Men's 110m High Hurdles, Women's 100m Hurdles, Men's 400m Hurdles, and 300m Hurdles. The Race Analyzer supports all four, plus a 300H → 400H projection tool and a 55m Indoor → 110m Outdoor formula.

How accurate is the 300H → 400H conversion?

The formula uses the Ross distance ratio (×1.333) plus a fitness-level fatigue factor: +3.5s for a well-conditioned 400H specialist, +4.5s for a trained athlete, +5.5s for a developing athlete with limited 400H-specific training.

It's a planning tool, not a guarantee — conditioning and race-specific fitness are the dominant variables beyond raw speed.

What are the Critical Zones in 110m?

CZ #1 is the cumulative time after Hurdle 3 — it measures the quality of the acceleration phase and first three hurdle clearances. CZ #2 is after Hurdle 8 — it captures the peak velocity window and the athlete's ability to maintain speed through the critical mid-race interval. CZ #3 is the finish line.

Athletes who lose time disproportionately in CZ #1 have a start and transition problem. Those who lose it in CZ #2 have a conditioning or rhythm problem. The app shows both clearly.
